# Empower, not replace

**Nomos Ludens builds technology to extend human capability without hiding where human authority lives.**

AI may assist. Automation may execute. Systems may suggest. The human role must remain legible wherever authorship, judgment, responsibility or final authority matter.

This principle is not anti-automation. It is a design constraint for responsible automation.

## 1. Human authority must remain identifiable

A system may accelerate work, reduce friction and automate execution, but it should remain clear who can decide, approve, reject, override and take responsibility for consequential outcomes.

## 2. Capability over substitution

We prefer tools that help people create, understand, operate, verify and decide more effectively. Removing repetitive work is useful; removing the human capacity to understand or intervene is not a default goal.

## 3. Assistance must preserve verification

Useful assistance does not end at producing an answer. Users need enough visibility, context and control to inspect results, identify failure and contest what the system proposes.

## 4. Local control when centralization adds no real value

When a task can reasonably remain on the user's device, local execution, local storage and direct ownership are preferred. Centralization should have a concrete operational reason.

## 5. Automation must be bounded and observable

Automated actions should be specific, authorized and verifiable. A system that can do more is not necessarily better than a system whose authority is deliberately constrained.

## 6. Reality over simulated success

A green build, plausible output, mock environment or agent report is not the same thing as a working system. Operational claims should be grounded in evidence from the environment that actually matters.

---

## What this does **not** mean

**Empower, not replace** does not require a human to manually perform every step.

It does not reject agents, automation, generative AI or autonomous execution.

It asks a narrower question:

> **Which human capability is being extended, which operation is being delegated, and where does authority remain?**

That distinction matters.

---

## How this appears in our projects

- **Kuan** — automation can organize, communicate and assist, while the Guardian retains final authority over confirmations and cancellations.
- **Héstia** — controlled actions are designed to be specific, authorized and verified rather than exposing unrestricted remote power.
- **OmniTreco** — browser and device capabilities are combined to give the user more direct computing power, with local execution whenever practical.
- **Kódice** — reading data and progress remain local-first, with synchronization as an optional layer rather than a prerequisite.
- **Book Composer** — software supports the work of structuring and producing a book; it does not claim the authorial role.

These projects differ in domain. The constraint is the same:

> **technology should increase agency before it increases dependence.**
